{# The garment and weather sprite for /clothes and the dashboard strip. HOW COLOUR GETS IN HERE — read before editing. clones a into a shadow tree. A stylesheet rule can NOT reach inside that tree (a class="sil" selector here silently paints every garment default black — that bug shipped once already). What *does* cross the boundary is inherited values: currentColor and custom properties. So every shape is painted from a var() with a literal fallback, and the host element carries the assignment: .art[data-g="pants"] { --g-fill: …; --g-ink: …; --g-trim: … } Three properties do all of it: --g-fill (the fabric), --g-ink (outline and interior lines), --g-trim (the contrast detail — a cuff, a placket, a sole). The fallbacks mean a context that sets nothing still renders a readable outline drawing. Gradients live INSIDE the symbol they belong to, not in a shared . A gradient resolves custom properties where it is *defined*, so a shared one would only ever see :root — and the weather colours are declared per sky class (.sky-day-clear …), which is how one sun symbol can be amber on a pale afternoon sky and pale gold at midnight. Cloned into the shadow tree, the gradient inherits from the host instead. --wx-detail is the fidelity switch: details that turn to mush at the 26px ribbon size carry style="opacity:var(--wx-detail,1)", and the small contexts set it to 0. SIZES: the art box is ~46px at a 62px tile, so 1 unit ≈ 1.44px — and ≈0.8px in the ribbon. Nothing smaller than 1.2 units, no gap under 1.5 units, interior lines 1.0, outlines 1.4. Ids match clothing.py's GARMENTS. #}
